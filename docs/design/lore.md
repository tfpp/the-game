# Casino Royale — Lore & World

> **Document purpose:** This file defines the intended world, tone, and established lore for *Casino Royale*.  
> It should describe **what is true in the world**, not how the game is implemented.

## 1. Core Premise

*Casino Royale* takes place in and around **The Golden Crown**, a strange casino standing in the middle of a vast, decaying urban slum.

The Golden Crown is warm, inhabited, functional, and comparatively safe. Outside its walls is a city that looks as if it has not seen a good day in decades: dark buildings, failing infrastructure, wet concrete, flickering lights, abandoned vehicles, and streets that feel permanently hostile.

The game does not need a complicated player backstory. The player is simply whoever they want to be. Players are customers, residents, drifters, gamblers, scavengers, or whatever identity they choose to imagine.

The story exists to give the world character, mystery, and atmosphere. It should never get in the way of the game.

## 2. The Golden Crown

The casino is called **The Golden Crown**.

It is the center of the player experience and the safest place in the world.

### Established Rules

- Violence is not allowed inside the casino.
- The casino is completely safe from PvP and PvE combat.
- Players inside the casino are customers or residents, not soldiers on assignment.
- The casino does **not** send players into the slums.
- Leaving the casino is a choice made by the player.
- NPC employees may operate shops, gambling tables, services, and other casino functions.
- The casino's true ownership and deeper purpose are intentionally vague.

A future reveal about the casino's owner may become an important piece of lore, but the answer should remain undefined until deliberately established.

## 3. Casino Atmosphere

The Golden Crown should feel like faded luxury.

Its visual identity is inspired by an aging casino that has survived long past its prime:

- Dreary 1960s decor.
- Dated wallpaper, carpet patterns, fixtures, and furniture.
- Warm artificial lighting.
- A sense that the casino was once glamorous.
- Clean enough to remain habitable, but never modern.
- Comfort that feels slightly artificial when compared with the world outside.

The casino should not feel like a sleek modern resort. Its charm comes from age, familiarity, and decay.

## 4. The Slums

The slums surrounding the Golden Crown do **not** currently have a proper name.

That is intentional.

They should feel larger than the playable maps. The player should have the impression that the Golden Crown is surrounded by an enormous dead or dying city.

From the casino grounds, the city appears as a dark silhouette beyond fencing and barbed wire. Flickering streetlights occasionally reveal pieces of ruined buildings, wet streets, broken infrastructure, and abandoned urban spaces.

The city should feel hostile without requiring constant exposition about what happened to it.

### Environmental Themes

Common qualities of slum locations include:

- Darkness.
- Heavy rain.
- Wet pavement and puddles.
- Flickering or failing lights.
- Concrete, rust, grime, and damaged infrastructure.
- Tight spaces mixed with occasional large open areas.
- Abandoned vehicles and structures.
- A constant feeling that something may be nearby.

The exact history of the city is deliberately vague for now.

## 5. Known Slum Locations

### Parking Garage

A multi-story parking garage filled with abandoned cars, concrete pillars, ramps, puddles, and failing fluorescent lights.

Cars provide both visual clutter and useful combat cover.

The garage should feel like a place that was abandoned suddenly and then left untouched for years.

### Alleys

A dense maze of narrow city alleys between deteriorating buildings.

Heavy rain creates puddles and reflective surfaces. Dumpsters, corners, fire escapes, doorways, and other obstacles create opportunities for ambushes and rapid close-range fights.

### Future Locations

Additional destinations can become increasingly strange as the game expands.

The elevator does not need to obey realistic geography. At some point the doors might open into:

- A ferry.
- A rooftop.
- A subway platform.
- An industrial building.
- A strange underground structure.
- Something completely absurd, such as an alien UFO.

The world is allowed to become surreal. *Casino Royale* should not take itself so seriously that every location requires a realistic explanation.

## 6. The Elevator

The Golden Crown's elevator is the primary connection between the casino and outside locations.

Its behavior is intentionally mysterious.

A player enters the elevator in the casino, but the destination may change from trip to trip. The elevator can deliver players to different slum locations without needing a realistic explanation for how the geography works.

This is part of the game's personality rather than a puzzle that must be solved.

## 7. Tone

The desired tone is a mixture of:

- Urban decay.
- Dark comedy.
- Unease.
- Cheap luxury.
- Surrealism.
- Fast, violent action outside the casino.
- Safety and absurd normalcy inside the casino.

The contrast between the Golden Crown and the slums is more important than explaining every detail of the world.

## 8. Story Philosophy

Story should remain secondary to gameplay.

The player should be able to ignore nearly all lore and still enjoy the game.

Lore should primarily be communicated through:

- Environmental details.
- Strange locations.
- NPC remarks.
- Signs and objects.
- Small mysteries.
- Background events.
- Contradictions that make players curious.

Avoid long mandatory exposition, elaborate cutscenes, or a player backstory that tells players who they are.

## 9. Canon Rules

Unless intentionally changed later:

1. The game is **Casino Royale**.
2. The casino is **The Golden Crown**.
3. The surrounding slums currently have no official name.
4. The Golden Crown is a combat-free safe zone.
5. Players choose why they leave the casino.
6. The casino does not command or dispatch players into the slums.
7. The player character has no required canonical backstory.
8. The elevator can connect locations in ways that do not make geographic sense.
9. The world may become surreal or absurd without requiring a serious explanation.
10. The identity of the casino's owner remains unresolved.

## 10. Intentionally Undefined

The following should remain open until deliberately decided:

- Who owns the Golden Crown.
- Why the Golden Crown is still operating.
- What exactly happened to the surrounding city.
- How large the surviving population is.
- Why the elevator can reach impossible locations.
- Whether the elevator is technological, supernatural, or simply a joke the game never explains.
- The exact factions or hostile groups outside the casino.
- A final story or ending.
