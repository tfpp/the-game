Art direction: plausible 1960s–1980s casino interiors, with PSX/GoldSrc geometry economy and realistic textures. Warm walnut, burgundy upholstery, brass, brushed steel, amber/green glass and restrained natural wear. Realism comes from silhouette, proportion, construction and broad material variation. Avoid cartoon materials, decorative pixel-art dithering, excessive grime and noisy fine detail.

Budget geometry before modelling. Use the fewest triangles that preserve the silhouette, hollow cavities and functional structure. Tiny props should generally use roughly 12–80 triangles; simple furniture roughly 60–160, adjusting only for a clear silhouette or structural need. These are project targets, not historical engine limits. Count every component, including stored objects and hardware. Paint labels, stitching, grain, pressed ribs and small crimp marks instead of modelling them. Remove hidden faces when they do not leave a visible hole. Preserve logical continuous manufactured forms.

CRITICAL: Do not assemble curved manufactured shapes by stacking separate primitive blocks. Construct bodies, stems, bowls, frames and handles from deliberate profiles, loops and surfaces. Separate real components must fit at plausible construction joints. Match their contact surfaces, shading and material scale. No floating attachments, obvious gaps, coplanar overlaps, arbitrary block mounts or decorative intersections. Inspect critical joins in close-up from several oblique angles, rear and underside views; fix them before delivery. A handle needs a plausible continuous bend, clearance and attachment. The earlier bin handles were unsatisfactory: do not use them as approved examples.

Allocate native textures by physical size and expected viewing distance. Use 16×16 for tiny objects such as the cigar and martini glass; 32×32 for small props such as bottles, buckets and clocks. An ashtray must be no more than 32×32. Use 64×64 for medium furniture such as the compact cabinet and cocktail table. Reserve 128×128 for wall-scale surfaces and 256×256 for large models only. 256×256 is the absolute maximum. Each prop gets its own appropriate allocation; do not put every small prop in a 256 atlas. Earlier seating and bin files have historical larger allocations, which do not override these current rules.

Build indexed vertices, triangle indices, normals and UVs first. Export a deterministic flat UV template. Use ImageGen to paint material charts into that exact template. Never ask ImageGen to invent UVs or render the object into the atlas. The image must remain a FLAT TWO-DIMENSIONAL material map, with exact chart positions, boundaries, orientations and proportions. Cylinder walls unwrap to strips; labels are flat graphics. No perspective views, whole-object silhouettes, cast scene shadows, furniture illustrations or background props. Broad material reflections may be baked into strips, but should stay restrained and readable at native resolution.

Generate large painting guides only as intermediate references. Downsample to the allocated native dimensions. Preserve chart interiors, then deterministically extrude each chart's own edge texels into its gutter and unused margins. Do not surround islands with black padding unless that material is itself black. Use half-texel-safe UVs and nearest sampling. Inspect the textured exported geometry at native texture resolution for bleeding, seams, misplaced labels and inconsistent detail scale. A large source painting must never become the final engine texture by accident.

Use one exported mesh and one shared material/atlas per prop where practical. Components may be disconnected surfaces within that mesh when their real assembly requires it; a single mesh is not permission for visibly disconnected construction. Duplicate vertices only where needed for UVs or normals. Metres, Y up; favour front +Z. Origins should suit placement. Keep colour textures opaque unless the task explicitly needs transparency. For requested burning tips, use an isolated emission mask at the same native resolution; emission must not light the whole prop.
