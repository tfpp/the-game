MESH CONSTRUCTION — HARD REQUIREMENT
Build a coherent, intentionally modelled object. Do not create an asset by grouping independent boxes, cylinders, or other primitive meshes whose intersections remain visible.

Use continuous mesh construction for each manufactured part: shared vertex positions along connected surfaces, consistent edge flow, and deliberate silhouette changes. Handles, straps and curved trim must be continuous sweeps or shaped surfaces, not separate bar pieces.

Separate components are allowed only where the real object has separate components. Their scale, thickness, placement and attachment must be physically plausible. Every attachment must meet its receiving surface: no floating ends, unexplained gaps, accidental penetration, stacked end caps, or block-like joints. Weld adjoining surfaces where they should form one piece. For assembled parts, model a convincing mount, recess, overlap or contact seam.

Match normals, smoothing and texture scale across continuous surfaces. Keep deliberate hard edges and real assembly seams. Check all joins in close-up renders from multiple angles before delivery. A triangle budget is not justification for visibly crude assembly; simplify hidden detail and small bevels first.
