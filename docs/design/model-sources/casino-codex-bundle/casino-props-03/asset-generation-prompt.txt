Create actual low-poly 3D casino props from the 1960s–1980s, with PSX / GoldSrc geometry economy and realistic material textures. Deliver indexed vertices, triangles, UVs, normals, GLB, OBJ/MTL, native texture PNGs, and a preview of those exported meshes.

Use the smallest practical power-of-two texture for the object's physical size and viewing distance: 16–32 pixels for tiny props, an ashtray no more than 32×32, 32×32 for bottles and buckets in this study, 64×64 for a compact cabinet, 128×128 for wall-scale surfaces, and 256×256 only for large models. These are allocated sizes, not an instruction to put every object in a 256 atlas. Larger images may be temporary painting guides; the actual exported texture must have the specified native dimensions.

Geometry defines the silhouette, cavity and structural function. Spend triangles only where they change those features. Paint grain, seams, wear, labels and minor hardware. Do not subdivide for surface detail or add ornamental bevel loops. Set a small per-prop triangle budget before modelling; count all components in that budget.

CRITICAL CONSTRUCTION RULE: Model each manufactured form as a coherent continuous shape. Do not approximate curved or joined parts by stacking independent blocks. Separate components are allowed only where a real construction joint exists. Fit contact surfaces exactly, avoid gaps, intersections, floating mounts and coplanar overlaps. Match shading and material scale at joins. Inspect important joins in close-up from several oblique angles, including undersides and rear views, and correct them before delivery. Handles must follow a plausible real handle design, including continuous bend, clearance and attachment; omit optional hardware rather than invent a malformed substitute.

Build the mesh first and unwrap it deterministically. Export a flat colour-coded UV template. Have ImageGen paint material surfaces within that exact layout. The texture must contain only flat material charts: no 3D object render, camera perspective, object silhouettes or scene cast shadows. Cylindrical surfaces become rectangular strips. Place labels as flat graphics on their proper chart. Preserve chart boundaries, orientation and scale. Do not ask ImageGen to infer or invent the UV layout.

Realism comes from correct broad material colours and restrained wear, not excessive noise or polygon density. Use realistic brass, brushed stainless steel, green glass, paper and walnut veneer. Avoid cartoon materials and decorative pixel-art dithering. Judge detail at the native texture resolution, rather than in the large painting preview.

Downsample painting to the allocated native size. Extrude each chart's own edge texels outward into its gutter and unused space; never surround islands with black padding unless the material itself is black. Use half-texel-safe UV boundaries and nearest sampling. Keep the final atlas opaque. Reinspect the textured geometry for seams, bleeding, misplaced labels and inconsistent material scale.
