**Document purpose:** This file defines the intended design, atmosphere, progression, and gameplay identity of the Parking Garage slum zone.

## 1. Zone Overview

The **Parking Garage** is one of the first major slum destinations accessible from The Golden Crown.

It is a large, decaying multi-story concrete parking structure built around a **massive open central shaft**. The shaft runs vertically through the garage and is completely open to the sky, allowing players to see the skybox, rain, lightning, and the surrounding city from multiple floors.

The garage should be **at least five playable stories tall**.

Players begin on the **top floor** and work their way downward.

The deeper they go:

- Enemies become stronger.
- Enemy encounters become more dangerous.
- Loot becomes more valuable.
- The environment becomes darker and more oppressive.
- The risk of continuing increases.

The basic decision should always be:

**Do I keep going deeper for better loot, or leave with what I already have?**

The garage should combine the risk/reward structure of descending into a dangerous place with the fast movement and combat of an arena shooter.

## 2. Core Layout

The Parking Garage is built around a large rectangular or square **central shaft**.

This shaft is the most important visual landmark in the zone.

It should:

- Be open all the way to the sky.
- Be visible from most floors.
- Allow rain to fall through the entire structure.
- Provide views of the stormy skybox.
- Reveal portions of the surrounding ruined city.
- Help players quickly understand where they are in the garage.
- Create vertical sightlines between floors.
- Make the building feel much larger than a series of enclosed rooms.

The playable floors wrap around this opening.

Ramps, stairwells, broken barriers, maintenance paths, and other routes connect the floors.

The layout should support moving quickly rather than forcing players through narrow realistic parking-garage paths.

## 3. Vertical Structure

The garage should contain a **minimum of five playable parking levels**.

The player starts at the top and descends.

A simple initial structure is:

### Level 5 — Arrival

The highest playable floor.

This is where players first enter the garage.

It should be the safest part of the map and immediately introduce the garage's layout.

Characteristics:

- Light enemy presence.
- Low-value common loot.
- Large amounts of open space.
- Clear view of the central shaft.
- Strong visibility of the sky and rain.
- Easier routes and simple encounters.
- Enough cover for players to learn how cars and pillars affect combat.

This floor should give the player a moment to orient themselves before the difficulty begins increasing.

### Level 4 — Early Danger

The first meaningful increase in danger.

Characteristics:

- More enemies.
- Slightly stronger enemy types.
- Better loot.
- More damaged or abandoned vehicles.
- Tighter combat spaces.
- More opportunities for enemies to attack from different angles.

Players should begin to feel that descending is a conscious risk.

### Level 3 — Midpoint

The middle of the garage.

This should be where the zone stops feeling safe.

Characteristics:

- Stronger enemies.
- More frequent encounters.
- Better weapons and valuables.
- More vertical combat opportunities.
- Sightlines across the central shaft.
- Routes that allow aggressive players to move quickly between encounters.
- More environmental damage and failing infrastructure.

A player reaching this floor should feel that they have entered the dangerous half of the garage.

### Level 2 — High Risk

A hostile and increasingly damaged section of the structure.

Characteristics:

- Dangerous enemy combinations.
- High-value loot.
- More aggressive enemy placement.
- Darker lighting.
- More broken lights.
- More standing water.
- Heavier environmental clutter.
- Areas where enemies can attack from above or below.
- Fewer completely safe positions.

Players should need to rely on movement, weapon knowledge, and awareness rather than slowly hiding behind cover.

### Level 1 — Deep Garage

The lowest main parking floor.

This is the hardest standard section of the zone and should contain the most valuable normal loot.

Characteristics:

- Strongest regular enemies in the garage.
- High enemy density.
- High-value loot.
- Rare item spawn opportunities.
- Dangerous multi-directional fights.
- Dark, wet, damaged environment.
- Strong contrast between artificial lights and darkness.
- Heavy rainwater collecting from the open central shaft.

Reaching the bottom should feel rewarding.

The exact contents of the lowest floor can change over time, but it should always justify the increased danger required to reach it.

## 4. Difficulty Curve

Difficulty should increase primarily through **encounter design**, not by simply multiplying enemy health.

As the player descends, difficulty can increase through:

- Stronger enemy types.
- Faster enemies.
- More dangerous weapons.
- Groups with complementary abilities.
- Increased enemy counts.
- Enemies attacking from multiple elevations.
- Reduced safe space.
- More aggressive pursuit.
- More complicated combat geometry.

Enemies should still work within the fast-paced shooter design of *Casino Royale*.

Avoid turning deep floors into slow cover-shooting encounters where players must patiently wait behind a car.

The intended response to increased danger is:

**move faster, aim better, choose better routes, and use the environment more effectively.**

## 5. Loot Progression

Loot quality increases as the player moves downward.

A simple rule is:

**Depth = danger = value.**

### Upper Floors

Common loot:

- Basic ammunition.
- Low-value valuables.
- Common weapons.
- Small amounts of money.
- Basic consumables.

### Middle Floors

Improved loot:

- Better weapons.
- Larger ammunition supplies.
- More valuable items.
- Less common equipment.
- Larger cash rewards.

### Lower Floors

High-value loot:

- Powerful or unusual weapons.
- Rare items.
- Large-value valuables.
- Significant amounts of money.
- Rare consumables or equipment.
- Items that are difficult to find elsewhere.

Players should understand the loot curve naturally.

Even without a UI explaining it, repeated runs should teach them that going deeper gives them better opportunities.

## 6. Risk and Player Choice

Players are never required to reach the bottom.

A garage run can be short.

A player might:

- Enter Level 5.
- Kill one enemy.
- Grab an item.
- Leave immediately.

Another player might descend through every floor looking for the most valuable loot.

Both are valid ways to play.

This supports the larger *Casino Royale* philosophy that entering the slums should not feel like committing to a long formal raid.

The Parking Garage should constantly tempt the player to go **one floor deeper**.

## 7. Combat Philosophy

Combat should follow the game's fast arena-shooter direction.

The garage is not intended to play like a tactical cover shooter.

Cars, pillars, ramps, and concrete walls provide cover, but they should mainly be used to:

- Break line of sight.
- Redirect movement.
- Create ambush routes.
- Enable aggressive flanking.
- Support fast weapon changes.
- Create interesting jumps and movement paths.

Players should frequently be:

- Running.
- Strafing.
- Jumping.
- Changing elevation.
- Crossing ramps.
- Moving around vehicles.
- Fighting while repositioning.

Standing behind a car waiting for an enemy to expose itself should rarely be the optimal strategy.

## 8. Central Shaft Gameplay

The central shaft is more than decoration.

It should influence combat and navigation.

Possible gameplay uses include:

- Looking across the garage to spot enemies or players on another floor.
- Jumping across small gaps where appropriate.
- Shooting across vertical sightlines.
- Dropping between floors through intentionally designed routes.
- Seeing combat happening below before reaching it.
- Hearing gunfire echo from lower levels.
- Seeing enemies or players briefly cross openings on other floors.

The shaft should make the entire garage feel connected.

A player on Level 5 should occasionally be able to see or hear evidence of what is happening several floors below.

## 9. Rain and Weather

Rain is one of the defining visual elements of the Parking Garage.

Because the central shaft is open to the sky, rain falls directly through the structure.

Rainwater should:

- Fall visibly through the shaft.
- Blow partially into exposed parking levels.
- Form puddles near the opening.
- Collect more heavily on lower floors.
- Drip through damaged concrete.
- Reflect broken fluorescent lights.
- Make the bottom of the garage feel especially wet and cold.

The weather should create atmosphere without making combat difficult to read.

Players still need to identify enemies, projectiles, pickups, and movement routes quickly.

## 10. Lighting

Lighting should deteriorate as the player descends.

The upper levels may receive some natural storm light from the sky.

Lower levels increasingly rely on old fluorescent fixtures.

Lights can:

- Flicker.
- Buzz.
- Fail intermittently.
- Illuminate small sections of otherwise dark floors.
- Reflect off puddles.
- Create strong silhouettes.

Darkness should enhance the mood, but it should not become a substitute for difficulty.

Important combat information must remain readable.

## 11. Environmental Identity

The garage should feel abandoned, damaged, and neglected.

Environmental elements can include:

- Abandoned cars.
- Wrecked cars.
- Concrete support columns.
- Parking barriers.
- Faded lane markings.
- Broken payment machines.
- Old signs.
- Maintenance rooms.
- Rusted pipes.
- Exposed electrical equipment.
- Puddles.
- Dripping water.
- Trash.
- Oil stains.
- Broken fluorescent fixtures.
- Bent railings.
- Cracked concrete.
- Graffiti.
- Views of distant ruined buildings.

The environment should suggest history without requiring written exposition.

## 12. Vehicles

Cars are major pieces of combat geometry.

They should:

- Break sightlines.
- Create temporary cover.
- Form narrow movement lanes.
- Make open parking floors more visually interesting.
- Provide locations for loot.
- Create recognizable landmarks.

Some vehicles may eventually support searchable trunks, interiors, or containers.

Vehicles do not need to be drivable unless that is deliberately added later.

## 13. Navigation

Players should be able to navigate the garage quickly after learning it.

Each floor should have recognizable landmarks.

Navigation tools can include:

- Floor numbers painted on walls.
- Different damaged areas.
- Unique vehicle clusters.
- Colored structural markings.
- Distinct lighting failures.
- Signs.
- Graffiti.
- Views through the central shaft.

The layout should be complicated enough to support fights and exploration but not so confusing that players regularly become lost.

## 14. Sound

Sound should reinforce both atmosphere and verticality.

Important sounds include:

- Heavy rain.
- Water dripping.
- Distant thunder.
- Fluorescent electrical buzzing.
- Wind passing through the open shaft.
- Car alarms or electrical failures used sparingly.
- Footsteps echoing through concrete.
- Gunshots reverberating between floors.
- Distant enemy noises.
- Combat sounds coming from above or below.

Players should sometimes hear danger before they see it.

## 15. Multiplayer

The vertical structure should make encounters with other players unpredictable.

A player may:

- See another player several floors below.
- Hear fighting and choose to descend toward it.
- Hear fighting and decide to leave.
- Race another player toward high-value loot.
- Attack from another ramp or elevation.
- Temporarily cooperate against stronger enemies.

The deeper floors should naturally concentrate players who are willing to accept greater risk for greater rewards.

## 16. Replayability

The garage should remain useful after many visits.

Replayability should come from:

- Variable enemy encounters.
- Variable loot.
- PvP encounters.
- Multiple routes between floors.
- Different weapon combinations.
- Rare loot spawns.
- Players choosing different depths each run.

The layout can remain recognizable while individual runs still produce different situations.

## 17. Design Rules

Future versions of the Parking Garage should preserve these principles:

1. The garage contains **at least five playable stories**.
2. The player begins at the **top** and primarily progresses downward.
3. A large **central shaft is open to the sky**.
4. Players can clearly see the skybox and rain through the shaft.
5. Rain physically and visually connects the floors.
6. Difficulty increases as the player descends.
7. Loot value increases as the player descends.
8. Going deeper is optional.
9. A short run is just as valid as a full descent.
10. Combat remains fast and movement-heavy.
11. The environment supports arena-shooter movement rather than slow cover shooting.
12. The garage should feel vertically connected.
13. Darkness and weather must not damage combat readability.
14. The bottom floor should feel meaningfully more dangerous and rewarding than the top.

- Exact return/extraction routes.

- Whether a basement exists below Level 1.
- Whether a boss or special encounter can appear at the bottom.
